## Executive conclusion

Workrr has a credible path to capital, but there is no honest “easy government grant” for a general AI consultancy. The highest-probability stack is:

1. recover cash already earned through federal and Arizona R&D tax credits;
2. pursue a $15,000–$50,000 Arizona/CDFI microloan if Workrr has at least one year of revenue;
3. use free Arizona capital-readiness and SBIR assistance;
4. apply for the next Arizona Innovation Challenge round after confirming team eligibility and entity good standing;
5. submit an NSF Project Pitch for a narrowly defined Workrr One technical innovation;
6. apply for cloud/vendor credits immediately to replace cash expense;
7. use customer-funded pilots and federal contracting as faster “seed capital” than federal R&D awards;
8. pursue equity only after separating the scalable Workrr One product story from the services business.

The most important distinction is between:

- **Workrr services:** production AI systems, integrations, workflow redesign, private deployment, and engineering. This can support loans, contracts, and revenue, but is rarely grantable by itself.
- **Workrr One product/R&D:** governed private AI operations infrastructure for mid-market organizations, including durable workflows, evaluation, DLP, approvals, auditability, model governance, and customer-owned deployment. This can support AIC, SBIR/STTR, R&D credits, accelerators, and equity.

## Business and fundable R&D profile

The current Workrr site describes a founder-led AI/software engineering company that builds production agents, automation, retrieval, data platforms, integrations, and private/in-customer-cloud deployments. It also identifies Reclaira as an internal production software lab. The repository describes Workrr One as a Cloudflare-native private AI operations framework for mid-market organizations without internal AI engineering teams.

The strongest R&D narrative is not “we use AI.” It is:

> Workrr is developing a governed execution and evidence layer that lets resource-constrained mid-market organizations safely deploy stateful AI processes inside customer-controlled cloud environments, with measurable reliability, privacy, human accountability, rollback, and provider portability.

Potential research questions that are sufficiently technical:

- Can a policy-aware execution layer maintain tenant isolation, bounded data retention, human approval, and deterministic audit evidence across synchronous, queued, stateful-agent, and long-running AI workflows?
- Can release-pinned evaluation and runtime evidence detect model/process regressions before fleet rollout while keeping inference cost practical for mid-market firms?
- Can automated DLP and memory-governance controls prevent sensitive-data persistence while preserving enough grounded context for useful agent work?
- Can provider-neutral model routing meet task-specific quality, latency, privacy, and cost constraints without loss of reproducibility?
- Can workflow telemetry identify recurring operational defects and recommend process redesign, not merely automate the existing workflow?

For grants, Workrr should propose one research hypothesis, baseline, measurable technical risks, experiments, and success thresholds. It should not submit a product roadmap or consulting proposal dressed as research.

### 1. Federal qualified-small-business R&D payroll tax credit

This is the closest thing to attainable non-dilutive cash because Workrr may already have created the qualifying activity. The IRS permits a qualified small business to elect up to **$500,000** of research credit against payroll taxes. The election must be made on a timely filed original return using Form 6765, then applied using Form 8974.

Likely Workrr qualified-research buckets:

- employee W-2 wages for developing and testing Workrr One;
- cloud compute and model/inference costs that qualify as research supplies or rented computing under applicable tax rules;
- eligible U.S. contractor research, generally subject to statutory limitations;
- systematic experimentation involving architecture, performance, reliability, privacy, evaluation, and technical uncertainty.

Not automatically qualified:

- routine feature work after uncertainty is resolved;
- customer configuration, sales, marketing, management, and support;
- foreign-performed research;
- duplicative funded work or work paid for by a customer where Workrr lacks financial risk/substantial rights.

**Action:** commission an R&D-credit study now, organized by project and technical uncertainty—not a percentage-of-engineering-payroll estimate. Preserve Git history, issue records, test results, architecture decisions, payroll, contractor agreements, and cloud invoices.

Primary sources: [IRS payroll R&D credit overview](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/qualified-small-business-payroll-tax-credit-for-increasing-research-activities), [IRS claim instructions](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/research-credit-against-payroll-tax-for-small-businesses), [Form 6765 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i6765.pdf).

### 2. Arizona R&D tax credit

Arizona provides an income-tax credit for increased qualifying research conducted in Arizona. A company with fewer than 150 worldwide full-time employees may seek a partial refund when the credit exceeds Arizona tax liability, subject to ACA certification and the annual cap. The **maximum refund is $100,000 per taxpayer per tax year**.

The catch: the ACA says the **2026 refundable cap is fully allocated**. This does not necessarily eliminate the underlying nonrefundable Arizona credit. For a 2027 refundable application, prepare actual-cost documentation in 2026 and file on the first business day permitted, before filing the corresponding Arizona return.

Primary source: [ACA refundable R&D credit](https://www.azcommerce.com/incentives/research-development-tax-credit/rd-refundable-tax-credit/).

### 7. NSF America’s Seed Fund (SBIR)

NSF is the best federal mission fit because it funds nearly all technology areas and accepts Project Pitches without waiting for a narrow agency topic. Its current topics include scalable analytics, machine learning, decision support, knowledge/data management, and novel software systems.

Eligibility includes:

- U.S. small business with fewer than 500 employees;
- at least 50% equity owned by U.S. citizens or permanent residents;
- all funded work performed in the United States;
- PI employed by Workrr at least 20 hours per week and meeting minimum project commitment.

Process:

1. take the Project Fit Assessment;
2. submit a required Project Pitch at any time;
3. wait roughly one to two months for an invitation decision;
4. if invited, register in SAM.gov/Research.gov and submit a full proposal;
5. expect a funding decision roughly six months after proposal submission.

SBIR.gov describes typical Phase I awards as **$50,000–$275,000** for 6–12 months; as of April 2026, agencies may make Phase I awards up to **$323,090** without an SBA waiver. The actual NSF solicitation controls.

#### Recommended Project Pitch

**Working title:** Evidence-Governed Runtime for Reliable Private AI Workflows

**Technical innovation:** a provider-neutral execution and evidence architecture that maintains reproducible releases, privacy constraints, human-approval boundaries, and measurable performance across stateful and asynchronous AI processes deployed inside a customer-controlled cloud account.

**Technical objectives:**

1. Design a formal execution/evidence model spanning synchronous, queued, durable-agent, and long-running workflows.
2. Quantify policy enforcement and tenant-isolation failure rates under concurrency, retries, partial failure, and model/provider changes.
3. Develop release-pinned evaluation and staged-rollout methods that predict production regression with bounded inference cost.
4. Validate on two operational workflows using de-identified or synthetic datasets, with explicit baselines.

**Candidate success metrics:**

- zero cross-tenant or prohibited-data persistence events in an adversarial test suite;
- at least 99.9% trace reconstruction across injected failure modes;
- statistically meaningful reduction in undetected workflow regressions against conventional prompt/version management;
- bounded evaluation cost per release;
- measurable reduction in deployment/operations effort for design partners.

**Commercial proof to collect before pitch:** three letters of support, at least two pilot candidates, pricing hypothesis, market sizing from bottom-up account counts, and evidence that existing orchestration/LLMOps tools do not solve customer-owned mid-market governance as proposed.

Primary sources: [NSF application process](https://seedfund.nsf.gov/apply/get-started/), [NSF topic areas](https://seedfund.nsf.gov/what-we-fund/), [SBIR funding/eligibility overview](https://www.sbir.gov/apply), [current award thresholds](https://www.sbir.gov/about).

## Revenue as seed capital
