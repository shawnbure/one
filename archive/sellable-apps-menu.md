# Sellable Apps — What a Real Buyer Actually Wants

**Status:** Draft v0.1
**Point:** Nobody below the C-suite buys "a catalog" or "a knowledge layer." They buy a small named app that kills one painful task they do every day. Same catalog underneath — sold as doorknobs, not cathedrals. **Land one team on one app → expand → then the platform pitch goes to the C-suite.** Bottoms-up.
**Companions:** [apps-and-plugins-catalog.md](apps-and-plugins-catalog.md) · [company-model-reality-check.md](company-model-reality-check.md)

---

## How to read this
Each app: the **name a person gets instantly**, **who buys it**, the **pain in their words**, and the **metric it moves**. All run on the same catalog — but each is sold standalone.

---

## Sell to the SALES team (champion: a rep; buyer: sales manager)

**Account Brief** — *"Everything known about this customer on one screen before every call — pulled from your email, CRM, and calendar automatically."*
- Pain: "I spend 30 minutes prepping for a call, or I wing it and look unprepared."
- Moves: prep time ↓, win rate ↑.

**Deal Radar** — *"Get pinged the moment a deal goes quiet or a follow-up slips."*
- Pain: "Deals die because someone forgot to follow up and nobody noticed."
- Moves: pipeline slippage ↓.

**Meeting Prep & Recap** — *"A briefing before every meeting; clean notes, action items, and CRM updates after."*
- Pain: "I forget what was said and never update the CRM."
- Moves: rep admin time ↓, CRM hygiene ↑.

---

## Sell to SUPPORT / CUSTOMER SUCCESS (buyer: support/CS manager)

**Answer Assistant** — *"Every support ticket, answered from your past cases, docs, and emails — drafted for the agent."*
- Pain: "Agents dig through five systems to answer one ticket."
- Moves: handle time ↓, first-contact resolution ↑.

**Customer History** — *"One screen with everything that ever happened with this customer."*
- Pain: "A new rep takes weeks to get context; handoffs lose everything."
- Moves: ramp/handoff time ↓.

**Promise Keeper** — *"Never miss a commitment you made to a customer."*
- Pain: "We promised something in an email three weeks ago and dropped it."
- Moves: SLA breaches ↓, churn ↓.

---

## Sell to FINANCE / BACK OFFICE (buyer: finance/AP manager) — hardest ROI to argue with

**Contract & Invoice Reader** — *"Pull the dates, amounts, and terms out of contracts and invoices automatically."*
- Pain: "Someone retypes this by hand into a spreadsheet all day."
- Moves: data-entry hours ↓ (easy dollar math).

**Obligation Tracker** — *"Know exactly what you've committed to and when it's due."*
- Pain: "We miss renewals and auto-renew into things we didn't want."
- Moves: missed-renewal penalties ↓.

---

## Sell to OPERATIONS / ANY TEAM (bottoms-up; champion: a team lead)

**Find It** — *"Search every system your company uses from one box."*
- Pain: "I know we have that document somewhere. I can't find it."
- Moves: time-wasted-searching ↓. (Most relatable; hardest to put a number on.)

**Onboarding Buddy** — *"New hires ask 'how do we do this here?' and get real answers from your own docs and history."*
- Pain: "New people interrupt senior staff constantly for weeks."
- Moves: ramp time ↓, senior-staff interruptions ↓.

**How Do We…** — *"Ask how your company does anything and get the actual process, with sources."*
- Pain: "The same questions get asked over and over; the answer lives in someone's head."
- Moves: repeated-question load ↓.

## Cross-role

**Reply Assistant** — *"Draft replies with the full history of that customer already in mind."* — response time ↓.
**Weekly Digest** — *"A Monday brief of what actually happened across the business."* — (skews manager/exec).

## The 3 wedges to lead with

Pick apps that are **acute pain + fast obvious ROI + championed by a non-exec + demo-able in 5 minutes**:

1. **Account Brief** (sales) — instant "wow" in a demo, clear revenue story, a rep will beg for it.
2. **Answer Assistant** (support) — measurable (handle time), a manager can buy it out of a team budget.
3. **Contract & Invoice Reader** (finance) — the ROI is literally hours × wage; nobody argues with it.

(**Find It** is the most universal but the vaguest ROI — great as a free "land" that pulls the others in, weaker as the paid lead.)

## Why this is actually one product (the expansion play)

Every app above reads from the **same catalog**. So:

1. Land **one app** with **one team** (small budget, one champion, quick win).
2. The catalog is now built from their connected data. Adding the **second app** for the **next team** is nearly free — the data's already there.
3. Two or three teams later, you walk into the **C-suite** and say: *"Four of your departments already run on this. Let's make it the company's system."* — **now** the platform story lands, because it's not a vision, it's a fact.

That's how you get past lower-level people: you don't pitch up, you **spread sideways until going up is obvious.**

## The "plug in → 2 hours → working apps" promise (keep it honest)

Some apps work in the first session; some need to watch the business for a while. The line = the two planes.

**Instant (hour-2, demo-able same day)** — run on freshly-connected content + on-demand tasks; **semantic plane** fills fast:
- **Find It** · **Answer Assistant / Ask** · **Contract & Invoice Reader** (works on any doc immediately) · **How Do We… / Onboarding Buddy**.

**Accumulating (sharpen over days–weeks)** — need **entity resolution + history** (structured plane); can't be good on day one:
- **Account Brief** · **Customer History** · **Deal Radar** · **Promise Keeper** · **Obligation Tracker** · **Weekly Digest**.

**On the "2 hours" number:** it's time-to-*first-working-apps on a prioritized slice*, not time-to-complete-catalog. Ingest most-recent/most-relevant first; instant apps work on what's indexed while the rest streams in; full depth builds for days. Don't promise all apps fully-baked at hour 2 — the demo would underdeliver.

**The honest two-beat pitch:** *"Plug it in — by this afternoon, [N] apps your team uses today. And they get sharper every day as it learns how your business runs."* Instant apps = the plug-and-play wow that closes the demo; accumulating apps = the compounding stickiness (the entity-resolved, temporal catalog no competitor has).

## Fit with the services model
Each app is also a **sellable engagement** — "we'll stand up Account Brief for your sales team in three weeks." Client-funded, concrete, low-risk. The app is the deliverable; the catalog is what you quietly build underneath, reused for the next app, the next team, the next client.
